AMULET ATLAS — WRITING A RECORD
===============================

One JSON file per node, in data/nodes/<type>/<id>.json. The schema is
schema/node.schema.json. Copy an exemplar and change every field:

  data/nodes/amulet/wedjat-eye.json      an amulet (rich: what / story / how / today, dating, object, against, kin)
  data/nodes/motif/eye.json              a sign that crosses traditions
  data/nodes/place/tha-phrachan.json     a place (geo, address, hours, tags)

Run  python3 tools/validate.py  until your records pass. Kin targets nobody has
written yet show as warnings; expect that while the set is still being built.

TYPES
  amulet     a kind of charm            tradition  the whole practice it belongs to
  motif      a sign cut into it         material   what it is made from
  practice   making, charging, wearing, feeding, retiring
  place      market, workshop, shrine, dig site, museum room
  person     maker, monk, excavator, scholar, collector
  org        museum, archive, research project, registry
  term       a word, with its root      art        a plate, a sign, a votive wall, a drawn sign
  story      a long read

THE THREE THINGS THIS SITE IS FOR
  1. Say what the thing IS, physically, so a reader could pick it out of a tray.
  2. Say what it is FOR, in the words of the people who carry it — reported, attributed,
     never adjudicated. This project does not test whether a charm works and does not
     tell a reader it does not. "Wearers say", "the text promises", "Elworthy recorded".
  3. Say WHERE and WHEN, precisely enough to put a dot on a map and a bar on a timeline.
  A record that cannot do all three is still worth writing; say which one it cannot do.

FIELDS THAT CARRY THE WEIGHT
  names.name    what the tradition calls it, romanised. names.native = its own script,
                with names.script and names.lang. names.said = how it is said out loud.
  text.what     one plain paragraph. Present tense. What it is, physically.
  text.story    narrative, told as narrative. Scenes, dates, names, excavations.
                Mark each stretch inline:
                  plain prose         = cited in this record's sources
                  *Tradition holds —*   general knowledge of the practice, hedged
                  *Inference —*         this project's own reasoning, labelled
  text.how      made, charged, worn, kept, renewed, retired. text.today = now, dated.
  kin[]         what another node IS to this one, said from here. One sentence a reader
                standing on this page wants — not "see also" but the fact that joins them
                ("the eye it carries is the same eye Naples cuts into coral"). The other
                record answers in its own words. Link only to cast-list ids.
                5-12 kin on a rich record, 3-6 on a small one. Reach across batches.
  dating        from_year / to_year in ASTRONOMICAL YEARS: 1 CE is 1, 1 BCE is 0,
                500 BCE is -499, 3000 BCE is -2999. to_year null means still carried;
                set living: true as well. label is the span in words, the way you would
                say it. method says how the date is known: excavated, inscribed, textual,
                ethnographic, art-historical, dated-object, undated. This field is what
                /time/ is drawn from — a record without it does not appear there.
  countries[]   ISO 3166-1 alpha-2, every country the practice is DOCUMENTED in, not
                every country somebody has sold one in. This is what /map/ is drawn from.
                Use the ones you can name a source for. Two is better than twelve guesses.
  object        form, materials[], worn[], inscription, script, made_by. Keys from
                data/vocab/forms.json, materials.json, worn.json. length_mm only where a
                source publishes a measurement — never a guess from a photograph.
  against[]     {harm, who, quote, source}. harm from data/vocab/against.json. `who` names
                whose claim it is: the maker, a named text, wearers, an ethnographer.
                A quote goes in quotation marks and carries a name. NO harm entry without
                a source. This is what /against/ is drawn from.
  holdings[]    where an example can be seen. An accession number goes in ONLY when you
                read it off a published catalogue page and can give that page's URL.
                No URL, no accession. `museum` from data/vocab/museums.json.
  restricted    set it where a tradition limits what outsiders may be told or shown. The
                site then prints your note INSTEAD of the detail — and you do not write
                the detail anywhere else in the record either. See the rule below.
  etymology     required on type=term, welcome anywhere a name has a root. Folk
                etymologies go in `folk`, labelled.
  sources[]     ids from data/sources/sources.json ONLY. Add your own to
                data/sources/new-<your-batch>.json in the same shape; build.py merges them.
                Wikipedia: s:wp-<slug>. A web page: s:web-<slug> with real title, publisher,
                url, and the date you read it. A book: title, author, publisher, year —
                never an ISBN you did not see.
  provenance    default tier + per-field overrides. Tiers: cited, harvested, tradition,
                inference, field. A date, a first attestation, an accession number, a
                price, a death: cite it or hedge it in the prose.
  images[]      leave empty. Put candidate Commons titles in
                x_commons_files: ["File:...jpg"]; tools/harvest_commons.py fetches them
                with their licences. Only CC0 / PD / CC BY / CC BY-SA / FAL.
  confidence    high / medium / low. needs_verification: true where a fact stays unchecked.

RESTRICTED KNOWLEDGE — THE RULE THAT OUTRANKS COMPLETENESS
  Some of this material is not ours to publish. Where a tradition restricts an object, a
  design or a rite to initiates, to one gender, to one clan, or to the dead, this atlas
  prints the restriction and stops. Write what is already public in the tradition's own
  publications and in its own framing; set `restricted` with a note saying who says so and
  where. Do not reconstruct a rite from scattered sources, do not print a consecration
  formula a tradition guards, and do not fill a gap with a neighbouring tradition's
  practice. A blank that says "this is not published, and here is who says it should not
  be" is a better record than a full one that guesses. Australia is mostly blank on this
  map for exactly this reason, and /story/what-this-atlas-does-not-print says so.

VOICE (house rules; validate.py enforces the banned list)
  BANNED WORDS, hard error: superstition, superstitious, primitive, savage, backward,
  witch doctor, mumbo jumbo, hocus pocus, trinket, knick-knack, exotic, authentic,
  inauthentic, genuine article, the real thing, mere, merely, just a charm, so-called,
  voodoo (the tradition is Vodou or Vodun; the spelling with two o's belongs in a
  quotation or a proper name and nowhere else).
  A quotation in quotation marks is exempt — so quote and attribute instead of asserting.

  EFFICACY IS NEVER THIS PROJECT'S SENTENCE. Not "it protects against the evil eye" and
  not "it is believed to protect" with nobody named. Write "a Neapolitan coral seller told
  Elworthy the horn takes the look meant for the child" or "the Qur'anic verse written on
  it is Ayat al-Kursi, which the tradition names as the verse of protection". Name who
  says it. Then stop.

  ARGUMENTS GET REPORTED, NEVER JOINED. Whether a mass-produced evil-eye bracelet is the
  same object as a village one, whether a dreamcatcher sold in an airport is anything at
  all, whether a Thai amulet market is devotion or trade: every one of those is a live
  argument with named people on both sides. Give both sides their names and their words.
  A factory charm gets described the same way a hand-cut one does.

  Write short. Lead with a verb. Cut "the" and "what" wherever the sentence survives it.
  Fewer adjectives, more verbs. Contractions are fine. Define every non-obvious word in a
  clause at first use — a reader who has never heard of faience should not have to leave.
  No sentence that announces the next sentence. No closing summary. Prices carry their
  date and their currency. Measurements carry their source.

PLACES
  geo from a published coordinate or the street address geocoded by hand; `precision` says
  which. address.country takes a two-letter code. tags[] EVERY tag names its evidence;
  the validator refuses tier tradition or inference on a tag. hours: THREE STATES, NEVER
  TWO — open, closed, and nobody published it. A day in neither list is unknown, and a
  guessed closure is worse than a blank because a reader flies on it.

FINISH BY
  running, from the repo root:  BUILD_DRAFT=1 python3 tools/validate.py
  Fix every ERROR that names one of YOUR records. Kin-target warnings for records other
  agents are still writing are expected and fine.
  Then report: how many records you wrote, which cast-list ids you could NOT source and
  why, and every fact you had to hedge.
