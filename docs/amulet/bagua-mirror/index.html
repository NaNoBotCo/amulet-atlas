said bā guà jìng

also: Pa Kua mirror · eight trigram mirror · feng shui mirror

amuletChinaChinaHong KongTaiwanconfidence mediumneeds checking

An octagonal or circular plaque with the eight trigrams set around a mirror at its centre. The mirror is flat, convex or concave, and which one it is decides what the object claims to do. It is screwed or hung above a front door or a window, facing out. It resembles a luopan, the feng shui compass, but it is not an instrument — nothing is read off it.

cited

Chinese 八卦 bāguà, 'eight trigrams', + 鏡 jìng, 'mirror'
A trigram is three stacked lines, each broken or unbroken for yin or yang; two states across three lines give eight figures, which is where the eight comes from. cited

The story

The thing it answers is a direction, not a person. In feng shui, sharp corners of neighbouring buildings and an open road running at a house count as sources of poison-arrows — Sha Qi, 煞氣 — and a bagua mirror is hung to deal with the arrow rather than the archer. A flat mirror is held to reflect it, a convex one to disperse it, a concave one to converge or absorb it, and so to alter the feng shui of the residence.

Modern bagua mirrors are described as evolved from ancient bronze-cast versions used by Taoist masters to perform Mirror Techniques, 鏡術, said to reveal or destroy evil spirits, or even to overcome natural calamities. The household version is the cheap end of that: an inexpensive, often self-administered quick fix to a specific feng shui concern.

Which is exactly where the trouble is. Whatever the mirror sends back goes in the direction the mirror faces, so pointing one at a neighbour's home is generally considered offensive and is a feng shui taboo — and the do-it-yourself nature of the object makes it easy to misuse for a private vendetta. Conflicts caused by offensive installations are well known in dense Chinese communities such as Hong Kong, and feng shui mirror feuds have been parodied in popular culture. Inference — this is one of the few charms in this atlas whose recorded quarrels are between neighbours rather than between believers and sceptics; the argument is not whether it works but where it is aimed.

How it is made and carried

Bought ready-made and mounted by the householder, above a door or a window, facing the corner or the road that is the problem. No consecration is described in the source read, and no specialist is required — which is the difference between this and the bronze mirrors of the Taoist masters it is said to descend from.

cited

Now

Sold in hardware shops, feng shui shops and online across the Chinese-speaking world as of 2026, in plastic and wood as often as in metal. A mirror over a doorway is also read by neighbours, which is why some cities treat their appearance as news.
