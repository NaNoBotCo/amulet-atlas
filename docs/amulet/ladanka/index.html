amuletsLadankaладанка

said LA-dan-ka

also: ладонка · ковчежец · incense pouch · Orthodox reliquary pendant

amuletThe Baltic and the Slavic landsDiasporaRussiaconfidence mediumneeds checking

A small pouch or a small hinged case hung at the neck on the same cord or chain as a baptismal cross. Cloth for the old kind, stamped or cast metal for the kind sold now, often with an icon on the lid. Inside goes incense, or a fragment of a relic, or a written prayer — commonly the Lord's Prayer, Psalm 90 or the Jesus Prayer. It is worn next to the skin and it is not opened for show.

cited

Russian ладан, 'incense, frankincense', plus a diminutive — the little thing you keep incense in
The same word once named a small brass censer, a sphere with a cross on top, set before the icons in the red corner of a house. The neck-worn pouch is the sense that survived. cited

The story

The Orthodox reference portal Azbyka very defines it in one line: a ковчежец со священными реликвиями в виде иконы, носимый на шее — a small reliquary with sacred relics, in the form of an icon, worn at the neck. The Russian encyclopedic entry adds the older layer: a fabric pouch, worn on the body, holding ладан, incense, and sometimes a bone from a saint's remains. Ivan Shmelev, in Bogomolye, gives it in a child's voice: "У бабушки ладанка из Иерусалима с косточкой... от мощей..." — grandmother has a ladanka from Jerusalem with a little bone in it, from the relics.

The positional rule is the first thing every Orthodox source states, and it is the whole argument in miniature. Её принято носить вместе с ним, но не вместо него: it is worn together with the cross, never instead of it. The cross is the baptismal object and cannot be displaced by this one.

The second thing every Orthodox source states is what a ladanka is not. Azbyka very is categorical: никаких оберегов и амулетов православный христианин носить на себе не должен — an Orthodox Christian should wear no protective charms and no amulets at all. The same entry says that wearing a ladanka with a prayer inside carries no sacred meaning without knowledge of the prayer and a personal appeal to God, and that treating an object with Christian symbolism as an amulet does the soul no good. The Russian encyclopedic account, describing what people do rather than what the Church teaches, records the object as functioning as both талисман and амулет, and notes that the pouches have carried protective signs.

Inference — set those two paragraphs side by side and this is the Orthodox twin of the Catholic argument on this shelf. In Rome the Church built a legal category — the sacramental — that lets a blessed object be devotional without being magical, and then spent centuries policing the edge of it. Here there is no such category to stand on; the instruction is simply not to. The object is still sold, still filled, still worn under the shirt, and the instruction is still printed.

The trade has moved with the money. Monastery and church shops sell ladanki in sterling silver and gold with enamelled icons on the lid; one Moscow Orthodox shop's catalogue page, read on 17 September 2026, listed twenty-five of them priced between 5,377 and 50,037 roubles. The cloth pouch with incense in it costs a fraction of that and is the older thing.

How it is made and carried

Bought at a church or monastery shop, or given. Filled with incense, a relic fragment, or a written prayer, and hung on the cross cord at the neck. The Orthodox sources this record cites do not describe a consecration rite for the pouch itself and this project has not gone looking for one; what they describe instead is where the relic or the incense came from, which is what carries the weight. Nothing is said about retiring one.

tradition

Now

Sold in Russian church and monastery shops in 2026 in every price band, from a fabric pouch to a gold locket. The Orthodox reference portal that explains what it is spends most of the entry explaining what it must not be taken for, which is a fair description of the object's position now.

Not to be confused with

  • Gau — a gau is a Tibetan Buddhist box, usually with a window showing the image inside; a ladanka carries an Orthodox icon on a closed lid and hangs on the same cord as a baptismal cross
