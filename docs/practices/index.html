Amulet AtlasPractices

Making, charging, wearing, feeding, retiring. A charm is a verb before it is a noun.

Charge (2)

Blessing at a shrine

1336 CE to now

Taking an object to where the power is, rather than bringing the power to the object. The move has two directions and this record keeps them apart.

Dispose (1)

Burying

1501 CE to now

Putting a charm in the ground and leaving it. Under a hearth, at the far corner of a property, in the threshold, in a well, in a wall.

Feed (1)

Feeding the charm

1501 CE to now

Inherit (1)

Inheriting

1830 CE to now

A charm that passes from one person to the next and gathers weight as it goes. The object is the same object; what changes is that it now carries a line of people.

Renew (1)

Renewing yearly

1764 CE to now

A charm with an expiry date. The old one is taken down, returned, burnt, buried or tied to a tree, and a new one takes its place on a fixed day.

Wear (6)

Counting beads

201 CE to now

A string of a fixed number of beads or knots, run through the fingers so that the hand keeps count while the mouth says something.

Foundation deposit

3000 BCE – 1964 CE

Objects buried under a building, at the start of it, by people who did not expect anyone to see them again.

Grave deposit

2686 BCE to now

Amulets put into a burial — on the body, in the wrappings, in the chest cavity, in the hand, around the coffin.
